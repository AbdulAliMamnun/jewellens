# Ring Specification Taxonomy — JewelLens Reference

Every specifiable attribute of a ring, organized by component. Tier markings indicate schema priority:
**[T1]** = already in RingParams or should be next · **[T2]** = phase-2 schema additions · **[T3]** = long-tail / template-specific, not schema material.

---

## 1. Centre Stone

### 1.1 Shape (cut outline) 
- **[T1]** Round (brilliant) — the default, ~60% of engagement sales
- **[T1]** Oval
- **[T1]** Cushion (square/rectangular with rounded corners; "chubby" vs "elongated" variants)
- **[T1]** Emerald (rectangular step cut, truncated corners)
- **[T1]** Pear (teardrop)
- **[T2]** Princess (square brilliant)
- **[T2]** Radiant (rectangular brilliant with cut corners)
- **[T2]** Marquise (boat/eye shape, pointed both ends)
- **[T2]** Asscher (square step cut, deep, almost octagonal face-up)
- **[T2]** Heart
- **[T3]** Trillion/trilliant (triangular), baguette (as centre — rare), rose cut, old European cut, old mine cut, kite, hexagon, shield, half-moon, portrait/portait cut, elongated hexagon

### 1.2 Cutting style (facet arrangement — independent of outline)
- **[T3]** Brilliant cut (57–58 facets), step cut (parallel facets: emerald/Asscher/baguette), mixed cut, rose cut (flat bottom, domed faceted top), cabochon (unfaceted dome — for opals, moonstone), old cuts (antique proportions)

### 1.3 Size & proportions
- **[T1]** Carat weight (0.25–5+ ct; drives mm dimensions via shape-specific lookup)
- **[T2]** Length-to-width ratio for elongated shapes (oval 1.30–1.50 typical; cushion 1.0 "square" vs 1.1–1.25 "elongated"; emerald 1.30–1.45; pear 1.45–1.75; marquise 1.85–2.10)
- **[T3]** Depth %, table %, girdle thickness, culet size (diamond-grading attributes — affect sparkle, not silhouette; irrelevant to a viewer)

### 1.4 Stone type / material
- **[T1]** Diamond (colorless), sapphire (blue), ruby, emerald
- **[T2]** Fancy sapphires (pink, yellow, teal, peach, white), morganite, aquamarine, moissanite, lab diamond (visually = diamond), tanzanite, spinel
- **[T3]** Opal, pearl, amethyst, garnet, topaz, tourmaline, alexandrite, onyx, turquoise, birthstone list
- **[T2]** Diamond color grade (D–Z) and fancy colors (yellow, pink, champagne) — visually representable as tint
- **[T3]** Clarity grade (FL–I3 — not visually meaningful in a viewer), certification (GIA/IGI)

### 1.5 Orientation
- **[T2]** North–south (traditional) vs east–west (contemporary) setting of elongated stones
- **[T3]** Tilted/offset/compass settings

## 2. Setting (how the centre stone is held)

### 2.1 Setting type
- **[T1]** Prong/claw — count: 4, 6 (8 rare); this is the classic solitaire head
- **[T2]** Prong style: rounded/ball, claw/talon (pointed), flat tab, double prongs (pairs at each position), V-prong (protects pointed tips of pear/princess/marquise)
- **[T2]** Bezel (metal rim fully encircling the stone) and half/semi-bezel (two arcs)
- **[T2]** Basket vs peg head vs trellis (the under-gallery structure; visible from the side)
- **[T2]** Cathedral (band arches rise toward the stone) vs non-cathedral
- **[T2]** Hidden halo (accent stones ringing the base of the head, visible from the side, not from top)
- **[T3]** Tension (stone held by band pressure, appears to float), flush/gypsy (stone sunk into the band surface), bar, channel-set centre, tension-style bezel, floating/suspended, cluster (multiple small stones forming one visual centre), illusion setting, toi-et-moi (two stones), three-stone as setting archetype (see 4.1), east-west bezel, chevron/V nest

### 2.2 Setting height
- **[T2]** Low-profile/flush vs standard vs high-set (affects side silhouette; "low profile" is a very common customer request)

## 3. Band (shank)

### 3.1 Width & thickness
- **[T1]** Width (1.3mm "whisper thin" – 8mm+ cigar band; 1.6–2.5mm typical engagement)
- **[T1]** Thickness/depth (1.2–2.5mm typical)

### 3.2 Profile (cross-section)
- **[T1]** Rounded/half-round (comfort dome), flat, knife-edge (peaked ridge)
- **[T2]** Comfort-fit (flat outside, domed inside), squared/European shank (flattened bottom prevents spinning), beveled, concave, D-shape
- **[T3]** Court profile, flat-court, bombe (domed heavy)

### 3.3 Band shape/path
- **[T2]** Straight, tapered (narrows toward or away from centre), reverse-tapered
- **[T2]** Split shank (band divides into two strands approaching the head)
- **[T2]** Twisted/rope (helical strands), bypass (ends pass each other rather than meeting), curved/contoured/chevron (shaped to nest with a wedding band)
- **[T3]** Freeform/bypass asymmetric, crossover, knot, infinity twist, open/negative-space shank, stackable-flat

### 3.4 Band stone treatment
- **[T1]** Plain, pavé (top arc) — currently paveBand
- **[T2]** Pavé coverage: quarter, half, three-quarter, full eternity; micro-pavé vs standard pavé vs French pavé (V-cut undersides)
- **[T2]** Channel set (stones flush in a metal channel), bead set, bar set, shared-prong
- **[T2]** Accent stone shape on band: round, baguette (channel-set baguettes are a distinct classic look), tapered baguette flanks
- **[T3]** Bezel-set band accents, scattered/confetti setting, hidden band stones (inner surface — "secret stones")

### 3.5 Band surface & edge detail
- **[T2]** Finish: high polish, matte/brushed, satin, hammered, sandblasted, florentine
- **[T2]** Milgrain (beaded edge detail — very common vintage cue)
- **[T3]** Filigree (openwork wire scrollwork), engraving patterns (wheat, scroll, leaf), rope edge, beaded edge, texture mixing (polished edges + matte centre), two-tone bands (different metal centre/edges)

## 4. Multi-Stone Architectures
- **[T2]** Three-stone (past/present/future; side-stone shapes: rounds, pears, tapered baguettes, half-moons; side stone size ratio ~1/4 to 1/3 of centre)
- **[T2]** Halo: standard, hidden (see 2.1), double halo, halo shape following stone vs contrasting (round stone in cushion halo)
- **[T3]** Five-stone, seven-stone, cluster/composite, toi-et-moi (two-stone bypass), constellation/scatter, eternity as centre-less architecture (already representable), gypsy multi-stone, ballerina (baguette skirt halo)

## 5. Metal
- **[T1]** Yellow gold, rose gold, white gold, platinum
- **[T2]** Karat: 10k, 14k, 18k, 22k (affects color saturation: 18k yellow is richer than 14k) — representable as material tint
- **[T2]** Two-tone (head in one metal, shank in another — extremely common: platinum head + yellow band)
- **[T3]** Sterling silver, palladium, titanium, tungsten, zirconium (black), Damascus steel, carbon fiber, rose/green gold alloys, vermeil, rhodium plating note (why white gold looks like platinum)

## 6. Fit & Sizing
- **[T1]** Ring size (US 3–13; international scales: UK letters, EU circumference mm, JP)
- **[T2]** Half and quarter sizes
- **[T3]** Wide-band size adjustment (+1/4 to +1/2 size for bands over 6mm), euro/squared shank sizing behavior, sizing beads, spring inserts, arthritis/hinged shanks

## 7. Ring Categories (drives which attributes matter)
- **[T2]** Engagement, wedding band (men's/women's), eternity, anniversary, stacking set, bridal set (engagement + contoured band), signet **[T3]**, cocktail/statement, promise, birthstone/family, men's dress, class ring — category shifts defaults (men's wedding: 5–8mm band, no centre stone, finish matters most)

## 8. Non-Geometric Specs (metadata, not viewer attributes)
- **[T3]** Engraving text (inside band), hallmarks, certification numbers, provenance, price, lead time, designer/collection name, style/SKU number — belong in the catalog schema (F2), not RingParams

## Schema roadmap implied by the tiers

**RingParams v1 (current):** shape (5), carat, stone color (4), band width/thickness/profile (3), prongs (0/4/6), halo, pavé, metal (4), ring size. → ~14 dimensions, covers the classic solitaire/halo family.

**RingParams v2 (worth building when Smiro's catalog demands it):** + princess/radiant/marquise/asscher/heart, L/W ratio, east-west toggle, prong style, bezel option, cathedral toggle, hidden halo, setting height, tapered/split/twisted shank, pavé coverage + channel/bead styles, finish, milgrain, three-stone architecture, two-tone, karat tint. → ~30 dimensions, covers an estimated 90%+ of custom engagement work.

**Never schema:** T3 items are either non-visual (clarity, certification), micro-visual (girdle thickness), or so design-specific they belong in bespoke templates (filigree patterns, freeform shanks) — the correct home for those is the per-studio Grasshopper archetype, which is the phase-2 onboarding work and the reason their files matter.

**Vocabulary note for the design-step system prompt:** even attributes the schema can't render should be *recognized* — "milgrain," "cathedral," "bezel" should come back as understood-but-not-yet-supported chips with correct definitions, never as confusion. A system that knows the vocabulary but is honest about rendering limits reads as expert; one that doesn't recognize "asscher" reads as a toy.
